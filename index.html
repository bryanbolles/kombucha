Kombucha Getting Started

What you will need

  1. kombucha scoby

  2. raw kombucha

  3. glass gallon container

  4. cheese cloth

  5. black tea (we use Lipton one gallon size tea bags or Tetley)

  6. pot to boil

  7. sugar

  8. Star san sanitizer This is where I get mine

  9. sanitizing bucket

  10. bottles (grolsh ~8 bottles per gallon)

  11. funnel

  12. picher to pour the kombucha into the bottles

  13. natural juice (no added sugar)


Start with 1 quart of water per gallon that you are brewing.  Add the water to your pot. Get that baby boiling.

At this point I get the bottles sanitized.  Put each bottle in the sanitizer and shake the water around inside, then dump it out and wipe off the outside. Do not rise the bottle or fear the foam it is safe and tasteless. 

Once all the bottles are sanitized get your Gallon of fermented kombucha take the scoby and about one cup to a cup and a half out.  Place them in a glass pitcher/jar to reserver for later.  Pour about 2 - 2 1/4 cups of the juice/flavoring liquid into your gallon jar with the fermeneted kombucha.  Sanitize a spoon and stir. Sanitize the pouring pitcher and funnel, then fill up the bottles.  Now seal bottles.

I place the sealed bottles in a tub and cover them with a towel (in case of explosions).  Then the bottles need to sit in a dark (not too cool) location for 6 days to carbonate.  Ours are in a basement closet.  After 6 days place the bottles in the fridge to stop the carbonation process.  Note:  Round bottles are designed to handle pressure.  We tried to carbonate in some glass flask shaped bottles & they exploded. 

When the water starts to boil take the pot off of the heat and place black tea in.  Cover pot and let the tea steep for 15 minutes.  After 15 minutes take out the tea bag(s).  I add some ice to cool the tea down quicker.

Add one cup of sugar to the bottom of the gallon jar.  Now add 1 quart of tea to the same jar.  Stir to disolve sugar.  (I learned not to add the sugar to the pot ~ it just makes everything sticky.)  Fill the jar with water until it is almost full.  Make sure the water is not too hot.  If the scoby is cold let it come to room tempature. Place the scoby and reserved kombucha in the jar and continue to fill the jar the rest of the way up.

Cover with cheese cloth and place in a dark location at room temperature. The tempature must be above 65F or else you will get mold.  After 8-12 days repeat.  During the summer we have shortened our brew time to 8 days, but in the winter (colder weather) we brew up to 12 days.  If your kombucha is too sweet, let it sit longer to eat more sugar.  Letting it sit for too long though will turn it more vinegary.
